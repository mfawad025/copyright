How a case moves.

Five steps from "someone stole my work" to "it's down." Real sequence, tracked with a case number the whole way through.

01Intake

Submit the case

You send the original work and every link where it's been used without permission. We open a case file and assign it a reference number.

02Verify

Ownership & evidence review

We confirm you hold the rights, timestamp the original, and document exactly how the infringing copy differs — or doesn't.

03Locate

Identify the right recipient

Every host, registrar and marketplace has its own DMCA agent and submission format. We find the correct one instead of mailing a generic address.

04File

Notice filed

A formal §512 takedown notice is submitted, citing the specific statute, URLs and proof of ownership. You get a copy of exactly what was sent.

05Track

Follow-up & resolution

If a host misses its response window, we re-file and escalate. Once the content is down, the case is marked closed and you're notified.

Before You File

Frequently asked.

How fast does content actually come down?

It depends entirely on the host. Major platforms with dedicated DMCA teams often act within 24–72 hours of a correctly filed notice. Smaller or overseas hosts can take longer, which is when follow-up filing matters most.

Do I need to prove I own the work?

Yes — a takedown notice requires a good-faith statement of ownership. We help you assemble that proof (original files, timestamps, publication history) as part of intake.

What if the other side files a counter-notice?

That's normal and doesn't mean the case is lost. We review the counter-notice and advise on next steps, including further legal escalation if needed.

Can you guarantee removal?

No service can guarantee a third-party host will act — but a correctly filed, evidence-backed notice is what gives a case the best odds, and that's what every WardMark filing is built around.
